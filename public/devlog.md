Hey! This is my development logs page, where I documented my entire design journey of Quacky (everything since March to now)

### Some context into why I made this (you will see later!)

At my previous school, there was alot of Online Cyberbullying, with a lot of it happening on social media like Instagram and Tiktok. Moreover, I've noticed that a lot of students aren't very aware of the consequences of talking to strangers or how to stay safe online. After the social media ban came into effect, it heavily impacted everyone's ability to connect with one another online, as we were all banned off plaforms in the name of "safety".

**So what?**

Don't get me wrong, the social media ban for under 16s definately has its place in keeping kids online. However, what happens after they are allowed back onto these platforms? Will teenagers have the needed skills to use social media responsibly?

**I don't think so.**

### 1st Week (9th March): Issue development

Previously, my old school would educate students about safe and responsible social media usage through lectures in the hall (i.e. when theres a big cyberbullying incident). I'm going to be realistic:
> No one is going to listen to a teacher talk if you force them to sit down for *hours* at a time and listen over and over again about why we can't post pictures of ourselves in uniform, how to private your social media accounts, and why cyberbullying is harmful.

Because lets be frank: we've all heard this talk thousands of times as a teen. No one will care, students aren't interested, serious, nor engaged whenever these talks happen.

I firmly believe that educating children should be through play. Think about it for a second. Games like Minecraft and Roblox have advanced so far, so why can't we, instead of educating through the same boring old lectures about how to behave online, we instead try a new approach and create games which incorporate the user's input to entertain them, allowing to naturally learn from the experiences?

Ironically, there have already been numerous studies that have proven that the gaming industry has some strong evidence that they can **educate through play** through cultural phenomena like Minecraft and Roblox. The gaming industry has already done this multiple times and have become the world's most compelling argument for what education software have long promised but struggled to deliver. So why is this?

Researching online, there have already been countless attempts to try and create so called "edutainment" software, whether its through games that:
- Teach you how to create an appropriate and safe profile
- Chatting appropriately online
- How to private your social media profiles (this is one of the most common).
- Behaving appropriately online
- How to report inappropriate conduct

However, have you ever seen these actually deployed at a school? I definately haven't.

The issue with all of these created examples is that they follow the same core principles that lectures do. I'd argue that most well known edutainment examples still rely on parent and staff compliance whereas real enjoyable, fun, and actually interesting games are built for student entertainment. My point here is that most games are still the same lessons, with the addition of interactive forms and buttons. For example,

- You have a social media game that teachers you about writing appropriate bios for your profile
- The game gives you a interactive UI which you write your bio in
- The game will then tell you to improve it in ways to make it safer

This completely contradicts the very nature of what a game should be. In fact, I believe this quote by Hack Club perfectly summaries the state of Edutainment:

> *We contend that educational games fail to deliver because the incentives of this market sector contradict the very nature of what a game should be.*
>
> *- Hackclub*

Games are supposed to be fun, enjoyable, and something that the user looks forward to coming back and participating in. The way the current edutainment industry leans on is still primarily education. Player retention on these types of games don't persist because they simply aren't interesting to play or interact with. The reason why Minecraft and Roblox have become successful is because they don't place teaching as the main objective. Instead, through the interactivity of games like Minecraft, **kids naturally learn from play**, and therefore I believe all edutainment games should follow these principles:

- Education occurs through instruction and practice
- Online videogames are natural vessels for practice
- Therefore, they are the perfect candidate for education.

In conclusion, after initial product research, I decided that I would try and compete with these social media games and make a experience myself. I decided that making a social media platform that educates teens through a realistic experience would be ideal, so I went with that approach.

### 1st Week (10th March): Product Inspiration

I got alot of inspiration for this project on a school trial my class participated in.

For business, we were the first class to try out **Banqer High**, a edutainment game where students compete against each other and try and build their net worth through a realistic banking game.

The game's core principles revolve around:
- Educating through a powerpoint generated for teachers
- After, we hop onto the **Banqer High** website, and complete the quiz
- Upon completing the quiz module for the specific lesson (i.e. Bank Accounts, Credit Cards, etc..), you would unlock features on the site like investing which you can then interact and play around with like you would in real life.
- Students would then experiment in-game with their savings and try and build their net worth through things like obtaining jobs, investing, property, etc.
- Student with the highest net worth wins.

![Banqer High](https://cdn.linus.my/qky/Q2LxhdxwHu6JlYSnTMyyfIptOthI2SAu/38b7eb06-6870-4788-801d-df291fdc15cf)

The key here is that **students are able to experiment and try out different things and see the consequences of their actions**. I decided that I would follow in their footsteps and create a social media platform, which slowly progresses as students learn more content and interact with the social media site to see real life consequences for themselves.

### What I will be making

Basically, I want to create a social media platform but for education. Basically:
- Quacky is a Social Media Platform, designed like a game so students can play around and experiment, whilst seeing real life consequences that their actions may/may not have.

This is how it will work:

1. School admins can self-host Quacky for their school, with their own domain such as ``https://quacky.qaci.eq.edu.au``.
2. Staff will invite students using the built in administrator features, which students will then click on to join Quacky socials.
3. When students click on the invite, they login, and then...
4. A user tutorial will popup, guiding the user through the platform. This guide will include a full walkthrough of all pages, and rules on how to behave online.
5. After completing the tutorial, students will have full access to the platform, so they can roam around, and see what other students are doing.

How the education part will work is that:
- There will be dedicated wellbeing lessons that students would already have as its a requirement in the Queensland Curriculam,
- Therefore, during these lessons, when teachers want to educate students about staying safe and reponsible on social media, they will present a Powerpoint which is created by Quacky Socials.
- After going through the course content, students will then be able to logon to Quacky, and complete the Quiz associated with the course material.
- The quiz will have various interactive activities including: Multiple Choice, Worded answers (guided by AI for feedback iterations), and general knowledge questions.
- Once completed, an AI model will judge the student's responses and determine if they are at the appropriate level set by the teachers.
- If so, they then pass that quiz and unlock the associated Module the quiz is for (for example, after completing the "Posting Responsibly" quiz, students unlock the ability to create posts on Quacky). They also gain currency which is set by teachers, which students can use to redeem items in the shop (i.e. if the school wants to add rewards like tuckshop vouchers).
- After unlocking Quacky modules, students can then use the social media platform like it is a real social space, for example, Instagram. Students can chat, hangout, or do practically anything they want on the platform.

Teachers will also be able to add their own custom course material into Quacky, with custom badges and modules to unlock.

Therefore, through this approach of learning through play, I believe that this idea will be able to both satisfy the requirements of school officials, and be enjoyable to students. Think of Quacky like Banqer High but for social media and online safety. I'm trying to gamify the experience of learning how to behave on social media.

Through this approach of learning, students can learn how to behave appropriately online by interacting with a safe and fun platform which only school students can access.

What better way to educate teenagers how to be responsible online then with a fully featured social media app with their friends, in a safe environment! Additionally, the Quacky app allows students to work on their wellbeing, with daily checkins and access to request help, and a anonymous stymie form!

### Deploying to production...

Woah! all the core features that I wanted for the social media beta are now completed. Right now, its not so much of a social media game, but more of just a typical social platform. After this feedback round, I will completely redesign Quacky from the ground up, with some new cool tech I am currently learning like ``turborepo``, ``trpc``, and ``create-t3-env``!

Basically I deployed it using your standard run-of-the-mill ``docker-compose.yml`` file:

```yml
services:
  quacky:
    container_name: quacky
    image: linuskang/quacky:latest
    restart: always
    env_file:
      - .env
    depends_on:
      - db
      - cdn
    networks:
      - quacky_network
    ports:
      - "3001:3001"

  db:
    container_name: quackydb
    image: postgres:16
    restart: always
    environment:
      POSTGRES_USER: quacky
      POSTGRES_PASSWORD: quacky
      POSTGRES_DB: quackydb
    volumes:
      - quacky_db_data:/var/lib/postgresql/data
    networks:
      - quacky_network
    ports:
      - "15432:5432"

  cdn:
    image: rustfs/rustfs:latest
    container_name: quackycdn
    restart: always
    environment:
      - RUSTFS_ACCESS_KEY=quacky
      - RUSTFS_SECRET_KEY=quacky
    volumes:
      - quacky_cdn_data:/data:rw
    command: server --address :9000 --console-address :9001 /data
    networks:
      - quacky_network
    ports:
      - "6000:9000"
      - "6001:9001"
    depends_on:
      - db

networks:
  quacky_network:
    driver: bridge

volumes:
  quacky_db_data:
    driver: local
  quacky_cdn_data:
    driver: local
```

I deployed this on my Proxmox VE mini pc located at home, however, I eventually the next week deployed this stack to my Hetzner VPS which I bought around this time, still using compose. I'm using Portainer to orchaestrate and manage the containers I was hosting alongside quacky.

### Version 2: Humble beginnings

- Sidebar didn't look the best
